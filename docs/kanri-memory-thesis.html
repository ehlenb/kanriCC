01 / Executive summary

The finding

Kanri has quietly built something no competitor has: a reconciled entity memory. The refresh-context handler reads up to thirty interactions per candidate, weights them CURRENT / RECENT / BACKGROUND by age, and writes a single narrative that resolves contradictions explicitly — "salary expectation updated to X (was Y at registration)." That is not a transcript summary. It is a current-state model of a human relationship, with its own correction history.

Three things are wrong with it.

  1. It is orphaned. Two of forty handlers read ai_context. requisitions.ai_context is written by refresh-context and read by nothing at all. Every other handler re-derives context from raw rows, which is slower, dumber, and inconsistent between features.
  2. It is manual. Refresh is a button. Memory that only updates when someone remembers to press a button is not memory.
  3. It has no outcome dimension. closed_reason, ccm_outcome, placed_fee_jpy, submission-to-interview conversion are all captured and never read back. The flywheel has no belt on it.

Meanwhile the other 38 handlers are one-shot generators — briefings, drafts, scripts, rejection emails. That layer is commoditising fastest. Spott ships weekly on $3.2M with a public roadmap and an MCP server. Bullhorn's Amplify runs ten autonomous digital workers and ranks candidates on 26 years of placement data. Recruiterflow bundles every current and future AI agent into one price. Kanri cannot win a generator arms race and should stop trying to.

The thesis: Kanri remembers. Everything else generates.

Japan's boutique agencies are 両手型 — one consultant owning both client and candidate. The named failure mode of that model is 属人化: knowledge trapped in one person's head, and the business stops when they are out. Kanri's memory layer is the literal answer to the defining operational problem of its exact target market, and neither the product nor the positioning currently says so.

Three numbers frame the opportunity. Japan's white-collar agency market was ¥449 billion in FY2024, up 12% year on year, across 30,561 agencies of which roughly 70% have ten or fewer staff. That is about 21,000 firms shaped exactly like Torch. Japanese agencies adopting AI report 1.3–1.8× expansion in per-consultant interview capacity, with three named targets: resume screening, 面談議事録 (interview minutes), and 推薦文作成 (recommendation-letter writing). Kanri does the first two adequately and does not do the third at all.

The strategic recommendation is not a feature list. It is a sequencing decision: stop adding generators, turn on the memory, and start recording outcomes — because outcome data is the only asset that cannot be backfilled, and every month it is not captured is a month of moat permanently lost.

02 / What Kanri does well

Six genuine strengths

Rare
Reconciled entity memory with recency weighting

Competitors store transcripts and summarise them. Kanri resolves conflicting facts across time and states the delta. Spott's Conversation Memory captures more channels; it does not reconcile. This is the single most defensible thing in the codebase.

Rare
Stage × outcome situational intelligence

SituationBanner is keyed on stage and ccm_outcome, and changes both its message and its action buttons. A CCM pass offers interview prep; a fail offers a rejection email and a close button. Most ATS products show a stage badge and stop.

Strong
Rule-based, explainable prioritisation

Six deterministic rules, each rendering its own reason string. Roughly 85% of recruiters want final authority over AI recommendations, and explainability is the top-cited trust barrier. A visible rule beats a hidden model here, and Kanri chose correctly.

Strong
Japan domain depth in the prompts, not the marketing

The nine-level Japanese proficiency scale, base_is_priority as a first-class field, ¥M formatting, counteroffer statistics, the domestic-to-foreign objection framework. This is encoded in handler prompts, not a localisation file.

Strong
Human-in-the-loop by construction

Every output editable, nothing auto-sends, notes_internal and notes_presentation firewalled from AI. Japan's 職業安定法 Art. 5-4 guidance requires exactly this posture: AI drafts, humans decide. Kanri is compliant by accident and should sell it on purpose.

Rare
Real bilingual operation

Full EN/JA UI plus on-demand translation of individual interaction notes, cached in full_notes_translated. A recruiter thinking in English can read a Japanese colleague's call notes. Almost nothing in this category does this.

03 / What Kanri does poorly

Twelve honest problems

Structural

Critical
The memory is write-only

ai_context is read by client-meeting-prep and spec-email. That is it. requisitions.ai_context has zero readers. The best asset in the product is switched off.

Critical
Search cannot answer a recruiter's actual question

Candidate search is ilike on name and company plus six exact-match dropdowns. No full-text over notes_interview, no semantic layer, no pgvector anywhere in the repo. "Who did I speak to about fintech product roles in Kansai?" is unanswerable.

Critical
Matching hits a hard wall at scale

advanced-search selects every eligible candidate and puts them all in one prompt. max_tokens was already raised 2500→8000 for ~60 candidates. At Torch's real database size this does not degrade — it fails.

Critical
No outcome feedback loop

closed_reason, ccm_outcome, placed_fee_jpy are captured. One handler reads them, scoped to a single client. Nothing learns across the database. This is Bullhorn's entire marketed advantage and Kanri has the schema for it already.

Serious
No task or follow-up object

Dashboard done/snooze lives in localStorage. It does not survive a browser change, is invisible to teammates, has no due date, and cannot represent "call him Tuesday." Recruiters live inside a follow-up queue and Kanri does not have one.

Serious
Zero business development surface

A company only exists in Kanri once it is already a client. No prospect object, no lapsed-client detection, no trigger events. Agency revenue is a function of BD, and the product has no place to do it.

Serious
Database re-engagement is impossible

20–30% of agency placements come from existing database records, and up to 70% of ATS candidates are never contacted a second time. Kanri has 200 seeded candidates with rich interview notes and no mechanism to resurface any of them.

Serious
O(n) engineering for O(n) questions

Forty one-shot handlers, each with its own endpoint, button and prompt. Every new question a recruiter might ask requires a new deploy. Spott shipped an MCP server and an Ask-AI box that answers arbitrary questions over the database.

Engineering debt that is now a strategy problem

candidates.$id.tsx is 5,579 lines. clients.$id.tsx is 4,309 lines. Together they are 51% of the frontend. Meanwhile src/hooks/, src/components/dashboard/, src/components/layout/, src/components/candidate/processes/ and src/components/candidate/registration/ are empty directories. CLAUDE.md §15 describes a component architecture that does not exist, and every agent that reads it starts from a false map.

Three more, briefly. CLAUDE.md is materially stale — it documents 19 AI endpoints when there are 40, and omits the import wizard, the Outlook add-in, Tavily enrichment, the entire ai_context system, the jobs/$id route, the test suite, and migrations 019–043. A wrong single-source-of-truth is worse than none, because agents trust it. Guarantee tracking is dead code — placement_guarantee_until exists and nothing reads it, while early turnover inside 3–6 months and the resulting refund exposure is a named industry problem in Japan. And the multi-user promise is unshipped: CLAUDE.md says the primary value of multi-user is seeing what teammates log, and there is no team feed anywhere in the app.

05 / Competitors: gaps

Where the field is weak

The universal gap: nobody owns what happens after the reply

Every well-funded player in this category is optimising the top of the funnel — find, rank, contact, sequence, schedule. Headhunt.AI says it outright: its job ends when the candidate replies, and it explicitly does not replace the ATS. Bullhorn's digital workers source, screen, match and outreach. Loxo sells profiles and contact data. Spott is broadest, and even Spott's differentiation is capture-and-unify, not judgment support.

The part of the job that decides whether an agency makes money — buy-in, submission quality, interview preparation, feedback chasing, offer defence, counteroffer, resignation, placement, guarantee period — is thinly served by all of them. That is exactly the span Kanri's processes table already models, stage by stage.

Specific weaknesses

  • Spott — limited mobile, no built-in external sourcing database, and a thin independent review base. Strategically: it is horizontal across every agency type in every market, which means it will never carry 推薦文, keigo register, or 両手型 operating assumptions.
  • Headhunt.AI — no pipeline, no client relationship model, no memory of anything after first contact. Its own performance figures are vendor-published and uncorroborated; treat them as directional. Its recommendation of no post-edit on ~95% of volume is also a philosophical fork away from where Japanese regulation and recruiter trust are heading.
  • Bullhorn — clunky UX and crashes are the recurring complaint, and ~20% renewal price increases are the single most common trigger for agencies shopping alternatives. Its outcome model is also a black box, which collides directly with the explainability demand.
  • Loxo — gates its best features behind pricing you cannot see until a sales call.
  • Vincere — this is Kanri's specific opening at Torch. Applications from Indeed and its own portal do not auto-parse into the ATS, the UI is unclear at a glance, and agencies are leaving over post-acquisition platform drift. The incumbent is actively annoying its own base.
  • PORTERS — dominant in Japan and generationally behind on AI. It will not build a reconciled memory layer.

Commodity functions — do not differentiate here. AI note-taking, AI email drafting, JD generation, CV reformatting, generic match scores, multi-channel sequences, enrichment waterfalls, contact finding. All of these are bundled into base tiers across the field by 2026. Spending Kanri's engineering on any of them is spending it on parity.

06 / What Japan needs

Nine things a Japanese boutique needs that a Western ATS will not build

  1. 推薦文 (suisenbun). A formal recommendation letter written by the consultant and submitted with 履歴書 and 職務経歴書 at document-screening stage. It has a conventional five-part structure — greeting, candidate basics, character and achievements with numbers, reason for changing jobs, closing. Japanese industry sources name it as one of the three highest-value AI targets in agency work. Kanri's submission-note produces an English client email. Different artifact, different register, different reader.
  2. 職務経歴書 formatting. Agencies routinely rewrite candidate career histories into the standard Japanese format before submission; 添削 (correction) is a headline service at every major agency. Kanri parses CVs and never produces one.
  3. 属人化 as the named enemy. Japanese operational writing on 両手型 identifies the failure modes precisely: single-owner dependency, task overload, missed steps. The stated tool requirement for 両手型 is single-screen visibility across jobs, candidates, matches and revenue. Kanri's memory layer is the answer and its positioning never names the problem.
  4. Duplicate-submission prevention. Japanese agency-ops guidance calls visibility into which client has already received this candidate critical infrastructure. Kanri excludes duplicates inside advanced-search and warns nowhere else.
  5. The multi-agency clock. Candidates earning ¥7M+ now register with several agencies simultaneously, so speed and exclusivity decide outcomes. Kanri has competing_interviews and treats it as a risk badge rather than a countdown.
  6. 内定辞退 defence. Offer decline runs roughly 15–20% at large firms and above 30% at SMEs, and the most-cited cause is a mismatch between what was described and what the conditions actually are. Offer stage in Kanri is a UI shell.
  7. Guarantee-period exposure. Early turnover inside 3–6 months and the refund obligation that follows is a named industry challenge. Kanri has the column and no behaviour.
  8. Keigo register as a control, not a translation. Headhunt.AI names keigo conjugation errors as the failure mode generic models hit, because they are trained mostly on English. Kanri translates after the fact; it should generate at a chosen register — 敬語 / 丁寧語 / casual — from the start.
  9. Regulatory posture as a feature. The APPI amendment was promulgated 17 July 2026, tightening enforcement with a surcharge regime for serious violations. Combined with 職業安定法 Art. 5-4, the operating rule Japanese counsel gives is explicit: AI produces drafts, humans make decisions. Kanri already works this way. Saying so, in the product and in the pitch, is worth more in Japan than another generator.

What not to localise. Do not build a BizReach or doda scraper. Headhunt.AI holds 4M Japan profiles under a registered 第4号 provider licence; Loxo holds 1.2B globally. Kanri will not win a profile-volume contest, and the APPI surcharge regime makes unlicensed candidate-data accumulation an unattractive place to be creative. Import what the recruiter already lawfully holds; do not acquire.

07 / Biggest unmet needs

Where recruiter hours actually go

The time evidence is consistent across sources even where the exact percentages differ. Recruiters spend somewhere between 30% and 52% of the working week on administration depending on measurement; a UK survey of 748 HR leaders in August 2025 put it at 17.7 hours of admin per vacancy — more than two working days per hire — of which 3.6 hours is application review, 2.5 hours is interview scheduling, and 3 hours is processing post-interview notes. Scheduling alone is repeatedly measured as consuming more time than recruiting itself.

The named tasks are mundane and identical everywhere: copying details into the CRM, cleaning half-complete profiles, checking whether a record already exists, fixing job titles, hunting for mobile numbers, updating notes, parsing CVs, and switching between systems to keep basics current.

The five unmet needs, ranked by value per hour recovered

  1. Logging that happens without the recruiter. Not a note-taker for calls — everything. Email both directions, meetings, and a voice route for the 30 seconds after a phone call. Kanri has an Outlook add-in and Recall.ai already; the gap is coverage and automatic reconciliation into memory.
  2. A follow-up queue that survives. The single highest-frequency recruiter behaviour is "come back to this on Thursday," and Kanri stores that intent in browser localStorage.
  3. Retrieval over everything the firm knows. Not matching — recall. The question is almost never "score these candidates"; it is "who do I already know who fits this, and what did they tell me last time?"
  4. Preparation that arrives unrequested. Kanri's briefings are excellent and require a recruiter to remember to press a button before a call. A brief that lands 30 minutes before a scheduled interaction converts a good feature into an indispensable one.
  5. Knowing which client conversation is worth having. Submit-to-interview below 33% signals a problem and above 40% is a reasonable target; the goal ratio for interview-to-offer is 3:1 to 4:1. Kanri holds every number needed to compute these per client and per hiring manager, and computes none of them.
08 / 34 opportunities

The full option set

Grouped by layer. Scores follow in §09; recommendations in §10–12.

Memory and retrieval — the substrate

  1. Automatic context refresh. Fire refresh-context from a database trigger on interaction insert, debounced, instead of from a button. Memory becomes a consequence of work rather than a chore.
  2. Universal context read. One shared context loader; every handler reads the reconciled narrative instead of re-deriving from raw rows. Turns 40 inconsistent features into 40 features that know the same things.
  3. Semantic and full-text retrieval. pgvector over notes_interview and ai_context, plus Postgres full-text search. Makes the firm's own knowledge searchable for the first time.
  4. Two-stage matching. Retrieve ~80 candidates by vector and hard filters, then rank with Sonnet. Removes the whole-table-in-a-prompt wall permanently.
  5. Ask Kanri. One agentic endpoint with database tools, replacing the pattern of one endpoint per question.

Outcome learning — the flywheel

  1. Structured outcome capture. A required reason taxonomy on every terminal process state: skills, English, Japanese, comp, culture, timing, counteroffer, candidate withdrew, hiring-manager preference.
  2. Client and hiring-manager scorecard. Per-contact conversion and rejection-pattern analysis. "Shimada-san has rejected three of your last four candidates on English level, not skills."
  3. Interview debrief capture. Structured post-CCM capture from both sides, feeding both the client scorecard and the candidate's memory.
  4. Placement post-mortem. After every placement and every loss, five lines of what worked, written back to client and candidate memory.
  5. Weekly recruiter review. What moved, what stalled, what you said you would do and did not, conversion against your own baseline rather than an industry average.

Japan-native work

  1. 推薦文 generator. Proper five-part Japanese recommendation letter in keigo, PREP-structured, built from ai_context plus interview notes plus requisition context. Editable, as always.
  2. 職務経歴書 builder. Generate the standard Japanese career-history document from the candidate record and CV, exportable as .docx.
  3. Keigo register control. Explicit 敬語 / 丁寧語 / casual selector plus EN/JA on every generated message, at generation time.
  4. Duplicate-submission guard. A visible warning anywhere a candidate enters a process: already submitted to Client X on this date.
  5. Competitive clock. Turn competing_interviews stage plus date into an estimated decision window. "You have roughly nine days."
  6. Handoff pack. One click: everything about this candidate or account for whoever takes over. The direct 属人化 answer.
  7. BizReach and doda import. Accept exports and pasted profiles from the platforms Japanese recruiters actually use.

Pipeline and revenue defence

  1. Offer-stage watch. Countdown, counteroffer risk, resignation-prep checklist, decline-risk flags.
  2. Guarantee and fallout tracker. Surface placement_guarantee_until; check-ins at 1, 3 and 6 months; fallout logged into the client scorecard.
  3. Database re-engagement engine. A weekly list of ten people worth recontacting, ranked by fit × staleness × likely life-event, each with a drafted opener.
  4. Job-change detection on your own database. Your placed candidate is now a hiring manager. The highest-value internal BD signal there is, and Kanri already has the data.
  5. Prospect object and BD pipeline. Target companies before they are clients; lapsed-client detection at nine months without a requisition.
  6. External BD trigger detection. Funding, expansion and executive-hire signals via the existing Tavily integration.
  7. Key-criteria spine. Promote requisition_conditions into the matching spine with must / nice / dealbreaker weights, split by hiring-manager-stated versus inferred.

Labour removal

  1. Two-way email auto-log. Extend the Outlook add-in to inbound, add Gmail. Logging stops being a task.
  2. Tasks and follow-ups as a real entity. Due dates, owner, origin (AI-suggested or manual), persistent snooze, team-visible.
  3. Pre-meeting brief that fires itself. Scheduled interaction or calendar event triggers the brief 30 minutes ahead, unasked.
  4. Voice capture. A 30-second post-call voice note becomes a structured interaction. Removes the largest single logging friction.
  5. Candidate self-update link. A tokenised link asking the candidate to confirm comp, availability and status. Kills the "are you still looking?" call.
  6. Interview scheduling. Calendar integration. The single largest measured admin sink, and pure commodity — build last or buy.
  7. Client portal. Shortlist sharing with client feedback captured as structured outcome data.

Trust and enablement

  1. Explainability panel. Every AI surface can name the exact records it read. Targets the top trust barrier and the Japanese regulatory posture at once.
  2. Team activity feed. The multi-user value CLAUDE.md promises and the app does not ship.
  3. Decompose the two mega-files. Not a feature. A precondition for everything above it.

10 / Build now

Top 10 — build now

  1. 01
    Turn on the memory: automatic refresh + universal read

    Debounced database trigger fires refresh-context on interaction insert. One shared loader gives every handler the reconciled narrative. Requisition context finally gets a reader.

    Why recruiters care
    Every brief, every draft, every score becomes consistent and current instead of each feature re-reading raw rows differently.
    Evidence
    The primitive already works. The gap is wiring, not invention.
    Competitors
    Spott captures more channels but does not reconcile. Nobody else models resolved current state with a correction history.
    Complexity
    Low. Trigger, queue, one loader module, 40 call-site edits.
    Moat
    Foundational. Every item below compounds off it.
  2. 02
    Semantic + full-text retrieval over the firm's own knowledge

    pgvector on notes_interview and ai_context, plus Postgres FTS. Then two-stage matching: retrieve, then rank.

    Why recruiters care
    "Who do I already know who fits this?" is the most-asked question in agency work and Kanri currently cannot answer it.
    Evidence
    Search is ilike on two columns. advanced-search puts the entire candidate table in one prompt and has already needed a token-limit raise.
    Competitors
    Spott shipped database-wide semantic search in April 2026. This is now table stakes, and Kanri fails at it structurally, not cosmetically.
    Complexity
    Medium. Embedding backfill, an embeddings table, a retrieval function, rewrite of two handlers.
    Moat
    Indirect but essential: without retrieval, accumulated data is inert.
  3. 03
    Structured outcome capture on every terminal state

    A required, short reason taxonomy when a process closes, plus captured interview debriefs. Ugly, unglamorous, and the only item here that cannot be added retroactively.

    Why recruiters care
    They do not, on day one. On day ninety it is the only reason Kanri knows something ChatGPT does not.
    Evidence
    Bullhorn markets ranking on 26 years of placement data as its central advantage. Kanri has the columns and no capture discipline.
    Competitors
    Enterprise players have volume. None gives a four-person firm a legible read of its own history.
    Complexity
    Low. A constrained field, a modal, a migration.
    Moat
    Highest in the document. Every month without it is moat permanently forfeited.
  4. 04
    推薦文 generator

    The formal five-part Japanese recommendation letter, in keigo, generated from reconciled memory and requisition context. Editable, as everything is.

    Why recruiters care
    It is written for every serious submission, it is genuinely time-consuming, and doing it badly costs document-screening pass rate.
    Evidence
    Japanese industry sources name 推薦文作成 as one of three priority AI targets alongside resume screening and interview minutes.
    Competitors
    No Western platform will build this. PORTERS could and has not.
    Complexity
    Low. One handler, one panel, keigo-tuned prompt.
    Moat
    Modest alone; high as proof that Kanri understands Japanese agency work.
  5. 05
    Client and hiring-manager scorecard

    Per-contact submit-to-interview and interview-to-offer conversion, plus rejection-reason patterns, computed from #03's data.

    Why recruiters care
    It changes behaviour immediately: which client to push, which hiring manager needs a different candidate profile, which account is quietly not worth the hours.
    Evidence
    Submit-to-interview under 33% signals a problem; over 40% is the target; interview-to-offer should sit at 3:1 to 4:1. Kanri holds every input and computes none.
    Competitors
    Bullhorn does this globally and opaquely. Per-firm and legible beats global and hidden for a boutique.
    Complexity
    Medium, and trivial once #03 exists.
    Moat
    Very high. Improves monotonically with use and is worthless to a competitor without the same history.
  6. 06
    Tasks and follow-ups as a real entity

    Due date, owner, origin, persistent snooze, team-visible. Replaces the localStorage dashboard state entirely.

    Why recruiters care
    "Call him Tuesday" is the highest-frequency intention in the job, and Kanri currently forgets it when the browser changes.
    Evidence
    Confirmed in the code: kanri_done_today and kanri_snoozed in localStorage.
    Competitors
    Everyone has this. It is pure parity — which is precisely why its absence is disqualifying in a bake-off.
    Complexity
    Low.
    Moat
    None directly. It is the surface every proactive recommendation must land on.
  7. 07
    Database re-engagement engine

    Weekly: ten people worth recontacting, ranked by fit × staleness × likely life-event, each with a drafted opener and a one-line reason.

    Why recruiters care
    It is the shortest path from existing data to an extra placement, which is worth more than any hour saved.
    Evidence
    Systematic rediscovery reportedly yields 20–30% of placements; up to 70% of ATS candidates are never contacted a second time. Recent rejects re-engage at 35–40%.
    Competitors
    Talent rediscovery exists as a feature name in Vincere and Loxo. Almost nobody makes it a weekly ritual with a reason attached.
    Complexity
    Medium. Depends on #02.
    Moat
    High. Quality rises with interview-note depth, which rises with usage.
  8. 08
    Duplicate-submission guard

    Visible warning wherever a candidate joins a process: already submitted to Client X on this date, by this person.

    Why recruiters care
    Duplicate approaches are embarrassing with clients and damaging with candidates.
    Evidence
    Japanese agency-operations guidance names cross-client submission visibility as critical infrastructure for both 両手型 and 片手型 models.
    Competitors
    Common in Japanese systems. Absent from Kanri outside the search handler.
    Complexity
    Very low. One query, one banner.
    Moat
    None. Build it because it is cheap and its absence is conspicuous to a Japanese buyer.
  9. 09
    Explainability panel

    Every AI output can show the records it read: these six interactions, this interview note, this requisition context.

    Why recruiters care

12 / Moonshots

Five that would make Kanri a different category

  1. M1
    The Agency Brain

    Firm-level memory across every recruiter and every year. "What do we know about Rakuten's Japan engineering org?" answered from twelve people's accumulated conversations, not one person's recollection. Every candidate and client memory rolls up into an account-level and market-level narrative that no individual could hold.

    Why it matters: this is the complete answer to 属人化, and it makes a four-person boutique operate with the institutional memory of a forty-person firm. It is also the thing that makes leaving Kanri unthinkable after two years. Risk: requires the memory layer, retrieval, and outcome capture all working first. It is the destination, not a project.

  2. M2
    Placement likelihood on your own outcomes

    Per-process probability with the reason stated in plain English, trained on this agency's history rather than a global corpus. "62% — your last four placements at this client all had Business-level Japanese or above; this candidate does not."

    Why it matters: Bullhorn's version is a black box over 26 years of everyone's data. Kanri's version would be small, transparent, and about you — which is the version a recruiter actually believes. Risk: needs 12–18 months of outcome capture before the numbers mean anything. Start collecting now regardless.

  3. M3
    Kanri as an MCP server

    Expose Kanri's memory and retrieval as tools. The recruiter opens Claude or ChatGPT and asks about their pipeline in natural language, with Kanri as the memory backend.

    Why it matters: it sidesteps the assistant arms race entirely. Kanri stops competing on model quality and competes on being the best-organised context in the recruiter's life. Spott shipped an MCP server in April 2026, and Headhunt.AI references MCP — the pattern is establishing. Risk: weakens the case for opening the Kanri UI daily. Ship it after the daily habit is real, not before.

  4. M4
    Ambient capture — the recruiter never types into a CRM again

    Calls, meetings and email captured automatically, reconciled into entity memory without a logging step. The activity log becomes a read-only consequence of working rather than a chore.

    Why it matters: this is the honest endpoint of "remove the labour between judgment and outcome." It also fixes the data-quality problem at its source — memory is only as good as what got logged. Risk: genuine privacy and consent exposure under APPI. It must be recruiter-controlled, per-channel, and auditable.

  5. M5
    Reverse marketing engine

    Take the strongest candidate currently available and generate the target-company list, the named contacts, and the pitch — using the agency's own placement history to predict which companies actually hire this profile.

    Why it matters: Japan is candidate-scarce with a job-to-applicant ratio above 1.2, which makes candidate-led business development structurally the right motion and almost nobody tools for it. It also converts Kanri from a system of record into a revenue-generating instrument, which changes what a firm will pay. Risk: needs the prospect object, outcome history and BD signals to exist first.

13 / What not to build

Deliberate refusals

  • More AI note-taking. Recall.ai is wired. Commodity across the field. Do not invest further.
  • Multi-channel outreach sequences. Correctly removed once already. Stay removed — it is Recruiterflow's core competence and a race Kanri has no reason to enter.
  • An external sourcing database or profile scraping. Headhunt.AI has 4M Japan profiles under a registered licence; Loxo has 1.2B globally. The APPI surcharge regime, promulgated July 2026, makes unlicensed candidate-data accumulation an actively poor place to be inventive.
  • A generic AI chat assistant with no tools. Without retrieval it is a worse ChatGPT attached to a smaller database.
  • Generic CV reformatting as a headline feature. Commodity. The Japan-specific 職務経歴書 is a different product and worth building; "make this CV look nice" is not.
  • Bidirectional ATS sync. Correctly deferred in CLAUDE.md. Keep it deferred permanently.
  • Analytics dashboards that do not change a decision. The jobs revenue chip is enough until the scorecard exists.
  • A full candidate portal. One tokenised self-update link is the 90% version at 5% of the cost.
  • A mobile app. It is Spott's stated weakness, not Kanri's opportunity. A mobile voice-capture route is worth having; a second full client is not.
  • Any further one-shot AI endpoints. This should become a rule in CLAUDE.md: a new recruiter question is answered by extending the context layer or the agent tool set, not by adding endpoint number 41.
14 / The moat

What should become hard to copy

Not AI. Four layers, ordered by how hard each is to replicate.

  1. Reconciled memory and its correction history. Competitors store transcripts. Kanri stores resolved current state plus the delta — what changed, when, and against what. Transcripts are a commodity; reconciliation over a two-year relationship is not, and it cannot be reconstructed from an export.
  2. The agency's own outcome model. This firm's conversion, per client, per hiring manager, per candidate archetype, with stated reasons. Bullhorn has scale; Kanri can have legibility. "Shimada-san has rejected three of your last four on English level, not skills" is a sentence a recruiter acts on today. A global model cannot produce it, and a competitor cannot import it.
  3. Japanese boutique workflow embedding. 推薦文, 職務経歴書, keigo register, 両手型 single-screen operation, duplicate-submission visibility, an explicit APPI posture. Global players will not build it because the market is too small; Japanese incumbents will not build the AI layer well. The gap between them is where Kanri lives.
  4. The recruiter's own behavioural model. Which candidates this person calls first, whose judgment calls proved right, which advice they took and what happened. Longest to build, hardest to copy, and it only accrues if decisions are logged as decisions.

What to build today so the moat exists tomorrow: record decisions and outcomes as first-class objects, not as side effects.

Every close gets a structured reason. Every stage advance records a why. Every "call this person first" recommendation records whether the recruiter followed it and what happened next. None of this is glamorous and none of it can be backfilled — which is exactly what makes it a moat rather than a feature.

15 / Architecture direction

Where the system should go

Kanri is currently a UI over forty prompt templates. It should become a memory system with a thin generation layer on top. Four structural moves.

1. A context layer, not a prompt-assembly convention

One module owns "everything known about entity X." Handlers request context; they do not query tables. This makes the memory layer load-bearing, makes explainability free (the loader knows what it read), and turns future context improvements into a single-file change rather than forty.

2. A retrieval layer beneath it

pgvector plus Postgres full-text search, exposed as a retrieval function. Matching becomes retrieve-then-rank. Search becomes semantic. Ask-Kanri becomes possible. Without this the accumulating data is inert, and the whole-table-in-a-prompt pattern hard-fails at real database size.

3. An event spine

Interactions and stage changes already exist as events. Give them consequences: refresh memory, create tasks, fire briefs, detect signals. Today the app reacts only to clicks. An event spine is what turns Kanri from responsive to anticipatory, which is the entire difference between the current product and the one described in §16.

4. Fewer endpoints, more tools

Collapse the generator sprawl toward a small set of composable tools behind one agentic surface, keeping the handful of high-value bespoke generators (positioning, 推薦文, submission note) as dedicated paths. The current trajectory is one endpoint per question, which does not terminate.

A challenge to the current thesis, for you to rule on. CLAUDE.md commits hard to displacing the incumbent ATS. The evidence points somewhere slightly softer as an entry strategy: Headhunt.AI explicitly does not replace the ATS and is winning in Japan; PORTERS has 2,800+ Japanese agency customers and a real staffing-supply switching cost; 70% of Japan's 30,561 agencies have ten or fewer people and run on PORTERS or Excel. Displacement is very likely the right end state and I am not arguing against it. But the wedge that gets Kanri into a Japanese boutique may be "make the pipeline data you already have finally useful," not "abandon PORTERS in week one." That is a positioning judgment, not a technical one, and it is yours to make — flagging it rather than assuming it.

16 / The ideal day

Tuesday, with the memory switched on

08:30

Kanri opens on nine items, not a dashboard. Each carries a reason drawn from something real. Item one: Tanaka-san, Offer, Salesforce Japan. Below it, in one line: his manager asked to meet him Friday — counteroffer risk. Last conversation Thursday, he said base matters more than total. Kanri knows that because he said it on a call three weeks ago and the memory reconciled it against what he said at registration.

Item four is not a candidate. It is Yamamoto-san at Mercari, whom the recruiter placed two years ago and who changed title to Engineering Manager last month. Kanri noticed. The suggested action is an intro message, already drafted, referencing the placement.

09:55

A brief appears unasked, thirty minutes before a call that is in the calendar. Not a summary of the file — a reconciled position. Since you last spoke: he mentioned his wife's job in Osaka, which contradicts the Tokyo-only preference from registration. His salary expectation rose to ¥16M in June. He has one competing process at CCM2, disclosed 12 days ago, so a decision window opens in roughly a week.

10:00

The call happens. Nothing is typed. It is captured, transcribed, reconciled into memory, and the salary figure that changed is flagged as changed rather than silently overwritten.

11:20

New requisition from an existing client. Instead of a search, a question: who do I already know for this? Kanri returns eleven people ranked with reasons that cite specific conversations, not keyword overlap — including two who were rejected for a different role at a different client eight months ago on grounds that do not apply here. It also flags that one of them was submitted to this client in March, so approaching them again needs a different framing.

13:40

Submission time. The 推薦文 is drafted in keigo at the right register, the 職務経歴書 is formatted, the client email names the hiring manager. Below the draft: Shimada-san has rejected three of your last four candidates on English level rather than skills. This candidate is High Business. Consider addressing it in the first paragraph. That sentence exists because someone recorded a rejection reason four times, not because a model was trained on 26 years of other people's data.

17:50

What the recruiter did not do today: log a single interaction, write a recommendation letter from scratch, rebuild context before a call, search for a CV, check which client already saw a candidate, or remember to follow anything up.

What Kanri learned today: one salary expectation moved, one location constraint changed, one client's rejection pattern got a fourth data point, one placed candidate became a hiring manager, and one recommendation was accepted while another was ignored.

What is waiting tomorrow: the Friday counteroffer conversation with a prepared script, the Mercari intro if it was not sent, and a re-engagement list that is now slightly better than last week's because the database knows five more things.

What stays human, permanently: deciding whether to take the brief. Judging whether the candidate is telling the truth about why they are leaving. Choosing which of two good candidates to push. Reading the room on a counteroffer. Telling a client something they do not want to hear. Kanri should never move into any of these, and CLAUDE.md should say so explicitly.
